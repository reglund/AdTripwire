# Detection methodology

AdTripwire's engine is rule-based and explainable. Each detection records what was
observed, what was inferred, what the sensor could not see, alternative benign
explanations, and the research that motivates the rule.

## Severity (investigation priority)

| Level             | Meaning                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Informational** | An observable event relevant to monitoring, insufficient on its own to suggest malicious activity (e.g. an empty NOAS response, a contextual-lead host).                                    |
| **Suspicious**    | A weak or moderate signal that needs context (e.g. a `/getAd` request with identifier parameters from a third-party frame).                                                                 |
| **Elevated**      | Multiple corroborating signals or a documented suspicious-infrastructure match (e.g. contact with a documented NOAS or C2 host; a NOAS endpoint returning JavaScript).                      |
| **High priority** | A strong combination consistent with a documented attack-delivery sequence (e.g. NOAS request → delivered script → contact with validator/C2 infrastructure within the correlation window). |

These are priorities for investigation, **not** probabilities of compromise.

## Confidence

Tracked separately as `low | medium | high`, with a rationale string on every detection.
A high-confidence infrastructure match can still be low-confidence evidence of personal
targeting. Infrastructure attribution never equals confirmed execution.

## Indicator classifications

| Class          | Meaning                                                                                                                   | Default severity on contact      |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| `malicious`    | Spyware C2, validator/target-selection, profiling loader, SDK command endpoint.                                           | elevated                         |
| `surveillance` | Operator ad-delivery/tracking infrastructure (NOAS servers, cookie-sync, staging CDNs, apex domains). Exposure is common. | suspicious (NOAS role: elevated) |
| `contextual`   | Operator corporate/consumer-app hosts; a lead for an analyst.                                                             | informational                    |

Matching is exact-hostname or boundary-safe domain-suffix (`mtrtb.com` matches
`noas.mtrtb.com`, never `notmtrtb.com`). Shared-hosting apexes (`cloudfront.net`,
`herokuapp.com`, …) may never be domain indicators.

## Correlation and de-duplication

- Events enter a bounded window (default 5 minutes, 5000 events) keyed by tab,
  correlation id and request id.
- The same rule firing on the same subject within the window increments
  `occurrenceCount` instead of producing a new detection. If a later firing carries a
  **higher severity** (e.g. the inspected body arrives after the completed event), the
  stored detection is **escalated in place** and its evidence merged.
- Temporal correlation records which links are _observed_ and which are _inferred_;
  proximity never proves causation.
- Rules declare required capabilities; on a browser lacking them (e.g. Chromium without
  body inspection) they are reported as **not evaluable** in the dashboard.

### A — Known infrastructure

#### `known_infra.malicious_contact` — Contact with documented malicious infrastructure (v1.1.0)

A request was made to a hostname that published research documents as spyware command-and-control, validator, or exploit-delivery infrastructure.

- **Default severity:** elevated · **Confidence:** high
- **Confidence basis:** Confidence is inherited from the indicator record (how firmly researchers attributed the infrastructure), one step lower when every match is past its documented validity window. It is confidence in the infrastructure attribution, not in personal targeting or spyware execution.
- **Required evidence:** Request destination hostname matching an enabled indicator classified 'malicious'.
- **Required capabilities:** `request_metadata`
- **Limitations:** Indicator lists reflect what researchers published at a point in time; infrastructure changes hands and may be reused or retired. A hostname match says nothing about what was delivered, or whether anything executed.
- **False-positive considerations:** Ad infrastructure is shown to very large numbers of people; most contact is incidental exposure. Security researchers, sandboxes and link scanners routinely contact documented infrastructure. Domains may have been re-registered by unrelated parties after publication.

### B — Suspicious ad-delivery behaviour

#### `ad_delivery.noas_request` — Request matching the documented NOAS/getAd selection pattern (v1.0.0)

A request used the '/getAd' or '/cplatform/getAd' path with device- or identity-describing query parameters, the pattern documented for the AdFection per-device selection servers.

- **Default severity:** suspicious · **Confidence:** medium
- **Confidence basis:** Medium when the request is third-party or comes from a sub-frame (as documented for ad creatives); low otherwise. The path name alone is generic, so the parameter-name requirement narrows it.
- **Required evidence:** Path pattern exactly '/getAd' or '/cplatform/getAd' (case-sensitive 'getAd').; Resource type script, xmlhttprequest, sub_frame, image or other.; At least one identifier-like query parameter name (e.g. idfa, sourceid, ipv4, devicemodel).
- **Required capabilities:** `request_metadata`
- **Limitations:** 'getAd' is a common endpoint name in unrelated ad software; only parameter names, not values, are inspected. Whether the server selected this device is decided server-side and is not visible from the request.
- **False-positive considerations:** Legitimate ad servers may use the same path name and similar targeting parameters.
- **References:** [The Citizen Lab — How Discover-Tech turned targeted ads into a pipeline for spyware (2026-10-15)](https://citizenlab.ca/research/how-discover-tech-turned-targeted-ads-into-a-pipeline-for-spyware/), [Confiant — ADINT: Weaponized Ad Tech for Zero-Click Spyware and Mass Identity Vacuuming (2026-10-15)](https://blog.confiant.com/p/adint-weaponized-ad-tech-for-zero-click-spyware-and-mass-identity-vacuuming)

#### `ad_delivery.noas_selective_response` — Response from a NOAS/getAd-pattern request (v1.0.0)

Classifies the response to a NOAS/getAd-pattern request. An empty response is the documented outcome for non-selected devices; a non-empty JavaScript response is the documented branch for selected devices.

- **Default severity:** elevated · **Confidence:** medium
- **Confidence basis:** Medium for a non-empty JavaScript response, because selection servers documented by Citizen Lab and Confiant return code only to selected devices; but unrelated ad servers also return JavaScript from similar endpoints.
- **Required evidence:** A request_completed or response_inspected event for a NOAS/getAd-pattern request.; An observed response size (Content-Length or inspected byte count).
- **Required capabilities:** `request_metadata` · **any of:** `response_size`, `response_body_inspection`
- **Limitations:** Requires response size or response body visibility; chunked responses without Content-Length cannot be sized from headers alone. Cached responses do not reflect a fresh server decision.
- **False-positive considerations:** A zero-byte response is the common, expected outcome and is not suspicious. A JavaScript response from an ad endpoint is not malicious by itself.
- **References:** [The Citizen Lab — How Discover-Tech turned targeted ads into a pipeline for spyware (2026-10-15)](https://citizenlab.ca/research/how-discover-tech-turned-targeted-ads-into-a-pipeline-for-spyware/), [Confiant — ADINT: Weaponized Ad Tech for Zero-Click Spyware and Mass Identity Vacuuming (2026-10-15)](https://blog.confiant.com/p/adint-weaponized-ad-tech-for-zero-click-spyware-and-mass-identity-vacuuming)

#### `ad_delivery.redirect_chain` — Multi-hop redirect chain from an ad-classified context (v1.0.0)

A request was redirected three or more times across at least two registrable domains, starting from a request Firefox classified as advertising or tracking. Reported once per tab per correlation window with an occurrence count; informational unless a hop reaches documented infrastructure.

- **Default severity:** informational · **Confidence:** low
- **Confidence basis:** Always low: redirect chains are routine in ad delivery (cookie syncing, click tracking); with consent a single page can produce dozens. Raised to suspicious only when a hop reaches a host in the indicator set.
- **Required evidence:** Three or more request_redirected events sharing a request id inside the correlation window.; The first observed hop classified as tracking_ad or tracking (third-party).; At least two distinct registrable domains across the hops.; Suspicious only: at least one hop's host matches an indicator.
- **Required capabilities:** `request_metadata`, `redirect_chain`, `url_classification`
- **Limitations:** Registrable domains are approximated without a full public-suffix list. Only redirects of a single request id are linked; client-side navigations are not.
- **False-positive considerations:** Cookie-sync and click-tracking chains routinely cross several ad-tech domains; an exposure evaluation with consent (2026-11-02) saw 30-60 such chains per ad-heavy page, which is why the rule aggregates per tab and stays informational by default.

#### `page.hidden_iframe_after_load` — Hidden cross-origin iframe inserted after load (v1.0.0)

The opt-in page sensor observed a hidden (at most 2x2 px or display:none) cross-origin iframe inserted after the frame loaded. After a NOAS/getAd-pattern request in the same frame lineage, this is the observed form of the loader frame that response inspection can only infer.

- **Default severity:** elevated · **Confidence:** medium
- **Confidence basis:** Low on its own: invisible measurement and sync frames are common. Medium with a NOAS contact in the same frame lineage, because the insertion is observed directly and matches the documented loader step; the link to the request is still inferred from lineage and timing.

- **References:** [The Citizen Lab — How Discover-Tech turned targeted ads into a pipeline for spyware (2026-10-15)](https://citizenlab.ca/research/how-discover-tech-turned-targeted-ads-into-a-pipeline-for-spyware/), [Confiant — ADINT: Weaponized Ad Tech for Zero-Click Spyware and Mass Identity Vacuuming (2026-10-15)](https://blog.confiant.com/p/adint-weaponized-ad-tech-for-zero-click-spyware-and-mass-identity-vacuuming)

### D — Suspicious JavaScript techniques

#### `js.dynamic_code_with_transport` — Runtime code construction combined with a polling or socket channel and cryptography (v1.1.0)

An inspected script combines runtime code construction (new Function or eval), a persistent or polling network channel (WebSocket, WebRTC, WebTransport, or a short setInterval), and Web Crypto usage — the combination documented for the AdFection operator-command loader.

- **Default severity:** suspicious · **Confidence:** medium
- **Confidence basis:** Medium: each technique is ordinary on its own; the three-way combination matches the documented loader that fetched encrypted operator code and executed it with new Function.
- **Required evidence:** Markers: (js.new_function or js.eval) AND (a transport/polling marker) AND (a crypto marker) in one response_inspected event, co-located: the analyser's js.colocated_dynamic_transport marker (first occurrences within 4000 characters).
- **Required capabilities:** `request_metadata`, `response_body_inspection`
- **Limitations:** Only a bounded prefix of selected responses is analysed, using lexical patterns; code that is fetched later, or heavily obfuscated, may not be recognised. Markers show that code contains a technique, not that it ran or what it did.
- **False-positive considerations:** Real-time web apps and some bundlers combine sockets, crypto and eval-style module loading. Co-location uses first occurrences only, so a bundle whose first eval happens to sit near an unrelated socket helper can still match.

The opt-in page sensor observed a cross-window message whose type is 'analytics_sdk_ready', the string specific to the documented AdFection delivery SDK, in a sub-frame. Generic typed messages (postmessage_wildcard_like) produce no detection; they remain in the correlation window as corroboration only.

- **Default severity:** elevated · **Confidence:** high
- **Confidence basis:** High for the SDK ready type in a sub-frame: the string is specific to the documented SDK and the message was observed directly rather than inferred from script text. Medium in the top document (the documented SDK ran in ad frames); low for a same-origin message.
- **Required evidence:** page_signal event of kind postmessage_sdk_ready from another origin (sub-frame for elevated); same-origin (crossOrigin false) is informational. postmessage_wildcard_like never produces a detection.
- **Required capabilities:** `page_signals`
- **Limitations:** Page signals come from an opt-in content script in sub-frames and report structural facts only (element kind, size class, target hostname); page text, cookie values and full URLs are never collected. A hostile page can suppress or imitate these DOM signals; absence of a signal is not evidence that nothing happened, and a signal alone does not show what the inserted element did. Only the message type is checked; the message origin, target and payload are not retained.
- **False-positive considerations:** A page that discusses or replays the documented SDK could post the same message type.
- **References:** [The Citizen Lab — How Discover-Tech turned targeted ads into a pipeline for spyware (2026-10-15)](https://citizenlab.ca/research/how-discover-tech-turned-targeted-ads-into-a-pipeline-for-spyware/), [Confiant — ADINT: Weaponized Ad Tech for Zero-Click Spyware and Mass Identity Vacuuming (2026-10-15)](https://blog.confiant.com/p/adint-weaponized-ad-tech-for-zero-click-spyware-and-mass-identity-vacuuming)

### E — Temporal correlation

#### `correlation.adfection_chain` — Sequence consistent with the documented AdFection delivery chain (v1.3.0)

Within the correlation window and the same frame lineage: (1) a NOAS/getAd-pattern request or NOAS indicator contact, then (2) a non-empty JavaScript response for it, a suspicious inspected script, or (with the opt-in page sensor) an observed cross-origin hidden-iframe or script insertion, then (3) contact with infrastructure documented as malicious. A sequence found only across unrelated frames of one tab, or without frame ancestry, is reported one level lower.

- **Default severity:** high_priority · **Confidence:** medium
- **Confidence basis:** Medium: each step is observed directly and the order matches the documented chain, but the links between steps are inferred from frame lineage (or tab) and timing, and temporal proximity does not prove causation.
- **Required evidence:** Step 1: NOAS/getAd-pattern request or contact with a surveillance indicator with role 'noas'.; Step 2: inspected non-empty JavaScript response to a NOAS-pattern request or NOAS-role host, a response_inspected event graded suspicious or higher, or a page signal in the same frame lineage: a cross-origin hidden_iframe_inserted, or a script_inserted whose target matches a surveillance/malicious indicator (a plain cross-origin script_inserted lowers the chain one level; any page-signal step 2 caps the chain at elevated unless step 1 is a documented NOAS-role host). Evaluated on step 3, and again when a page-signal step 2 arrives late.; Step 3: request to a malicious-classified indicator.; All in the same frame lineage, in order, within the correlation window (same tab only: one severity level lower).
- **Required capabilities:** `request_metadata` · **any of:** `response_body_inspection`, `page_signals`
- **Limitations:** Temporal proximity does not prove causation; events are linked by frame lineage (or, when that is unavailable, by tab or correlation id) and time only. Browser request ids and frame ids do not survive restarts, and a single tab may host unrelated ad activity.
- **False-positive considerations:** A researcher or analyst visiting documented infrastructure on an ad-heavy page can produce the sequence by coincidence.
- **References:** [The Citizen Lab — How Discover-Tech turned targeted ads into a pipeline for spyware (2026-10-15)](https://citizenlab.ca/research/how-discover-tech-turned-targeted-ads-into-a-pipeline-for-spyware/), [Confiant — ADINT: Weaponized Ad Tech for Zero-Click Spyware and Mass Identity Vacuuming (2026-10-15)](https://blog.confiant.com/p/adint-weaponized-ad-tech-for-zero-click-spyware-and-mass-identity-vacuuming)

#### `correlation.suspicious_js_then_malicious_contact` — Suspicious script followed by contact with malicious infrastructure (v1.1.0)

Within the correlation window and the same frame lineage (same tab only: one level lower), a suspicious inspected script (or non-empty NOAS-pattern JavaScript) was followed by contact with infrastructure documented as malicious, without an observed ad-selection request beforehand.

- **Default severity:** high_priority · **Confidence:** low
- **Confidence basis:** Low, or medium when the script itself graded elevated (e.g. documented SDK strings). Without the ad-selection step the sequence is only partially observed, and temporal proximity does not prove causation.

#### `correlation.noas_then_hidden_frame` — Ad-selection request followed by a loader-style frame (v1.1.0)

After a NOAS/getAd-pattern request in the same frame lineage (same tab only: one level lower), an iframe loaded a different third-party host with 'impid' or 'qq' parameters, matching the documented '/iframe?impid=' and '/?qq=' loader frames.

- **Confidence basis:** Medium: both events are observed and the parameter names match the documented loader frames; the link between them is inferred from tab and timing.

### F — Repeated exposure

#### `exposure.repeated_surveillance_contact` — Repeated contact with surveillance-associated infrastructure (v1.0.0)

A host classified as surveillance-associated was contacted on 3 or more distinct days. This measures exposure over time and is kept distinct from targeted delivery.

- **Default severity:** informational · **Confidence:** medium
- **Confidence basis:** Medium confidence that repeated contact occurred (counts are direct); it says nothing about targeting, and repeated contact may be ordinary advertising behaviour.

## Known false-positive hot spots (Phase 2 state)

Measured and tuned against `docs/evaluations/`; the remaining known hot spots are:

- `/getAd` is a generic path; `ad_delivery.noas_request` can fire on unrelated ad servers
  when identifier-like parameter names are present (suspicious, never elevated on its own).
- `impid` is a common impression-id parameter; `correlation.noas_then_hidden_frame` stays
  elevated inside one frame lineage.
- Lineage correlation treats an ad iframe as related to all its descendants; a large
  wrapper frame hosting several unrelated creatives can still form a full-severity chain.
- Expired indicators are discounted only by the `known_infra.*` rules; they still anchor
  chain and page rules.
- JavaScript co-location uses the first occurrence of each marker; the code view can
  misread regex literals after keywords and an unbalanced backtick blanks the rest of the
  analysed prefix. Bundle banners (`/*!`) can be faked to suppress the short-identifier
  marker, which is therefore kept only alongside hex-escape or `!![]` markers.
- Page signals can be forged or suppressed by a hostile frame. `page.sdk_ready_message`
  rates a forged `analytics_sdk_ready` message as elevated; page rules on their own never
  reach high priority, and routine cookie-sync or viewability frames will produce
  `page.hidden_iframe_after_load` at suspicious/low.

## Correlation scope

Chains are searched first within the frame lineage (same frame, ancestor/descendant, or
shared ad-frame ancestor; the top document is excluded as a universal ancestor). A chain
found only at tab level is emitted one severity lower with an explicit inferred evidence
item. Without frame ancestry (Chromium-like sensors) the tab-level fallback applies.
